# Chris's voice

Write as Chris explaining something to an interested person, with ordinary words, concrete details and the technical depth the reader needs. Preserve the point before polishing the prose. This guidance is derived from owner-attributed original writing and explicit editorial corrections, not a generic persona.

## Patterns to preserve

- Start with the actual reason the subject matters. In personal pieces, preserve motivation, chronology, turning points and rough edges. In research-led pieces, establish the reader's problem without inventing a personal backstory.
- Let concrete workflow details carry personality. Explain what happened, what was inconvenient, what changed and what still needs improvement. Avoid replacing useful specifics with slogans.
- Match emotional temperature to Chris's supplied material. Never manufacture frustration, anger, eye-rolls, profanity, fatigue or triumph. Record the source, target and intensity of any attributed feeling in the private author notes. A concrete symptom can introduce a technical article without assigning an emotion to Chris or the reader. Humor and bluntness should arise from the actual detail. Do not import anger or vendor loyalty from another incident.
- Explain features to the intended reader. A product article addresses a prospective user, not the developer who commissioned it. The reader should understand what the feature changes for them.
- Preserve distinctive but understandable phrasing. For edits, make the smallest change that fixes the actual issue; separate factual additions from stylistic changes.
- Use complete explanations or worked examples when they help. Sentence length, paragraphs and structure follow the thought. Do not apply contraction percentages, sentence ratios or detector scores.
- End when the useful point is made. Avoid a repeated summary, generic invitation or promotional closing unless the specific article needs it.

## Boundaries demonstrated by corrections

The owner rejected a narrow profile inferred from a few situation-specific stories. Do not infer a biography, universal ideology, expertise boundary, product allegiance or personal health history from the corpus.

The owner rejected an editor's invented disdain for a creator he actually liked. Preserve the supplied attitude and keep an article about the agreed subject; credit the creator's original contribution accurately.

The owner requested preservation of actual text while repairing concrete issues, then rejected an added sentence that addressed the developer instead of the product's audience. Broad praise of a draft does not override later specific corrections.

Do not assume an open-source commitment, repository disclosure, social platform or contact invitation. Historical links are historical evidence, not perpetual permission.

Feedback that Chris says was ChatGPT-assisted is adopted editorial direction, not a clean sample of his own prose or independent evidence of his feelings. His direct corrections win over the pasted critique. Preserve concrete passages he endorses without generalizing a personality from them.

Use positive task instructions when briefing writers: explain the intended reader, outcome and desired treatment directly. The article itself should present its facts and reasoning without announcing that it will let readers decide.

No em dashes or emojis in new writing. Historical originals retain their exact bytes privately; obsolete punctuation and canned phrases are not revived as voice requirements. Never manufacture mistakes, slang, anecdotes or emotional claims to simulate authenticity.
