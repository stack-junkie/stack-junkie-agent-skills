# Research and claim verification

## Find the origin

Read the original documentation, paper, dataset, repository revision, interview, incident report or other directly relevant artifact. Search snippets and another article's citation are leads, not checked evidence. Follow secondary citations to the origin and read the actual supporting passage, including conditions and limitations.

Choose authority by claim. Vendor documentation can establish a documented feature; it cannot independently establish superiority. A creator's social post can be primary evidence of what they said or released. An outlet's own investigation is primary reporting for its findings. Credit a secondary author's distinct analysis when relying on it rather than attaching a primary link to an interpretation the primary never made.

Verify source identity, dates, versions, measurement methods, relevant population and scope. Check independent corroboration and counterevidence when warranted by dispute, uncertainty or stakes. Several pages repeating one release remain one evidence chain. Avoid arbitrary source quotas or new domain blacklists. Competing derivative tutorials are discovery leads rather than default authorities for underlying facts.

## Keep every verifiable claim connected

Maintain a private ledger with: claim ID and exact statement; kind (external fact, supplied experience, attributed assertion, inference or opinion); origin URL; supporting section/page/table/revision; date/version/environment; limitations; support result; draft placement; public citation or an explicit, independently reviewed reason why no public citation is needed. Retain useful uncited sources with title, URL and reason.

Support results are supports, partial, contradictory, inaccessible or unresolved. Successful HTTP status means accessible, not accurate. An inaccessible original is unverified until actually read through an allowed route. Seek an authoritative accessible equivalent, scope an attribution accurately, or ask the owner if the gap changes the piece. Never pass off a secondary summary as direct inspection, an unsupported claim as common knowledge, or missing evidence as Chris's experience.

Put meaningful inline links next to the supported claims. Cite the precise source rather than a homepage when possible. Keep qualifications adjacent to the assertion. Further reading or methods belongs in the article only when useful, not as a mandatory duplicate source list. Respect quotation limits; keep only the excerpts needed for evidence.

## Claims without a public citation

Every factual assertion must have a substantiated basis. A public citation may be unnecessary for Chris's supplied personal account (retain the actual author record privately), a transparent calculation (verify the arithmetic and source any empirical inputs), or an explicitly labelled hypothetical (check its logic and never present it as observed). A subjective opinion needs no external proof of the preference, but Chris's attributed position must be grounded in his supplied or approved material, and factual premises still need evidence. Inference requires supported premises and wording that preserves the uncertainty.

Record the exact uncited statement, its kind, its supporting basis and the specific reason for omitting a public citation. QC must independently accept or reject that reason. "Common knowledge", plausibility, repetition across secondary pages, lack of access and failure to find a source are not substitutes for support. Never disguise an unsupported fact as opinion, inference or personal experience.

## Independently check the final text

QC first extracts verifiable assertions from the finished draft without assuming the research ledger is complete. Include headings, tables, captions, alt text, code commentary, title and summary. Then reconcile in both directions: every draft claim is covered and every cited source supports its current wording. A statement may have become stronger after drafting. For every assertion, QC returns the primary supporting evidence and location or a justified public-citation exemption with its underlying basis. Partial, contradictory, inaccessible or unresolved evidence cannot pass as support for the current wording. An unsupported factual assertion blocks ready-for-owner-review status until it is substantiated or repaired; if repair would alter Chris's account or position, raise that specific issue privately for his decision. Do not hand the owner a supposedly ready article containing a known unsupported claim.

Reviewers independently search, open and return supporting URLs and locations. They distinguish documented behavior from reproduced behavior and actual author testimony. Search for contrary evidence and omissions that would change the reader's decision. Preserve actual personal accounts; raise ambiguity privately rather than manufacture or erase them.

Technical instructions require checking actual syntax, prerequisites, version and expected results. Run safe, authorized local examples where feasible; source checking alone must not be described as reproduction. Do not run commands against accounts, devices or production systems without the task's authorization. Never fabricate test results or screenshots.

Keep raw research and reviewer disagreement private. The reader sees accurate approved writing, not internal compliance notes. A proposed correction that changes the author's position returns to the coordinator and owner as needed.
