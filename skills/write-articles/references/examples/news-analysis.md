# Bing's citation reporting adds context to the count

In June 2026, Microsoft announced four preview additions to Bing Webmaster Tools: Intents, Topics, Citation Share and Compare. The announcement described a global preview rollout, rather than proof that a particular account already had every feature. [Microsoft's announcement](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/6/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare/).

For an editor, the useful question is what decision these measurements can support. A citation total alone does not tell an editorial team which reader problem its article is helping to answer. The new views offer more ways to investigate that question, subject to their stated limits.

## What Microsoft announced

Intents categorizes grounding queries, while Topics groups related queries into broader subject areas. Microsoft says those classifications can remain broad during the preview. Citation Share expresses a site's share of citations for a grounding query, and Compare overlays a previous reporting period. [Microsoft's feature descriptions](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/6/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare/).

Microsoft explicitly distinguishes Citation Share from traffic share, a quality score or a competitor-domain report. [Microsoft's metric explanation](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/6/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare/).

## A more useful editorial question

Consider a hypothetical troubleshooting publisher. If a dashboard associates a repair article with a learning-oriented topic, the editor could inspect whether the page explains the failure as clearly as it explains the fix. That is a question prompted by a classification, not evidence that the heading must change.

The editor should then read the page, compare the proposed improvement with actual reader needs, and record the reason for any approved revision. A broad topic label should not rewrite the article's intent by itself.

## What a before-and-after chart cannot settle

A rise after an edit is an observation. It does not isolate the edit as the cause. Microsoft also notes that model changes and shifting demand can affect citation patterns. [Microsoft's comparison caveats](https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/6/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare/).

The proportionate response is to inspect the account's actual availability, save a dated baseline and annotate meaningful changes. Keep citation observations separate from visits and reader outcomes. The announcement gives publishers more context to examine, but the explanation of a result still needs evidence.
