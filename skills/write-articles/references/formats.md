# Article formats

Choose the form that serves the reader's task and agreed angle. These eight types are editorial choices, not a website taxonomy or required frontmatter enum. Map the chosen type to the destination's current schema when integrating an approved article.

The complete examples below calibrate coverage, reasoning and evidence. They are instructional articles written for this package, not the owner's writing, approved golden articles, or evidence of the owner's experience. Source-check dates are not promises of continuing accuracy. Reopen time-sensitive sources when adapting an example.

## Shared decisions

- Establish the reader's question, useful result and available evidence before choosing sections. A diagnosis and a personal opinion need different pacing.
- Put a concrete introduction before Key Takeaways when a takeaway block helps. A personal account should open with the event or position that gives it meaning and may need no takeaway block. Follow explicit destination requirements when applicable, and raise conflicts with the brief.
- Use the owner-preferred descriptive H2 style: name the actual symptom, question, decision or subject. Search vocabulary informs natural wording, without exact-query quotas or repetitive keyword variants. There is no heading quota, required FAQ, fixed article length, detector target or prose ratio.
- Finalize the title and description after drafting. Describe what the finished piece delivers. Keep the proposed summary within Chris's 160-character house ceiling, without distorting meaning. Do not describe that ceiling as a Google ranking rule.
- Link externally verifiable claims to evidence that supports them. Place citations beside the claims. Open original sources, preserve scope and date, and distinguish attribution, inference, opinion and genuine experience.
- Choose images after reading the draft, using authentic screenshots or precise diagrams where useful. Inspect each at reading size beside its text. These text-focused examples do not establish a site's hero policy.
- End when the reader has the promised answer or a clear account of what remains unknown. Related links, definitions and questions must earn their space.

Google says its AI search features do not require special writing or one ideal page length. Choose structure for the audience instead of dividing content into artificial fragments. [Google's AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide), checked October 4, 2026.

## Guide

**Reader task:** accomplish a defined outcome from a known starting point.

State prerequisites, environment and the final observable result. Put commands in execution order and explain substitutions before the relevant block. Include the failure branch likely to prevent completion. Separate documented behavior from commands actually run in a stated environment. Verification must exercise the promised result.

Useful shape: result and scope, preparation, ordered work, outcome check, relevant recovery. Combine sections when the task is small.

Quality question: can the reader finish without guessing a missing command, value or success condition? Avoid prerequisites hidden after the steps, invented output and unrelated comparisons before completion.

## Troubleshooting

**Reader task:** distinguish causes of a symptom and repair the relevant cause.

Start with the symptom, affected environment and a discriminating check. Branch on evidence. Prefer reversible diagnosis and the smallest correction. Explain what a passing check establishes and what still needs application-level verification. Preserve working state and useful diagnostic evidence.

Useful shape: symptom, decisive check, cause, bounded fix, confirmation, next branch if it persists.

Quality question: does the evidence justify the fix, and can the reader tell whether the original problem is solved? Avoid default reinstalls, untested exact error strings and declaring success from syntax alone. When a reader must match events, IDs or output, seek one authentic annotated example from an actual run, original documentation or a versioned upstream fixture. Identify that provenance visibly. A fixture does not demonstrate a live failure or successful repair. Never invent output to fill the gap.

## Reference

**Reader task:** look up a meaning, option, constraint or supported value.

State scope and authoritative version. Use a table only when its repeated fields make lookup or comparison easier; include meaningful exceptions. A list can be clearer for independent entries. Keep examples small enough to isolate the concept. A reference need not manufacture a narrative or teach an entire workflow.

Useful shape: scope, lookup material, worked interpretation, important exception, full specification link.

Quality question: can someone find the answer and avoid the nearest likely misinterpretation? Avoid tables without conditions, unexplained abbreviations and assumptions that implementations are identical.

## Comparison

**Reader task:** choose between alternatives for an identified use case.

Define the decision and apply the same criteria to each option. Separate vendor statements, specification differences and measurements. Give test conditions for empirical claims. Identify a documentation comparison as such. Explain when the recommendation changes.

Useful shape: decision, common criteria, evidence, tradeoffs, conditional recommendation.

Quality question: can the reader understand why an option suits their situation? Avoid unsupported overall winners, false certainty in evidence gaps and promotion determining the verdict.

## Review

**Reader task:** assess whether a particular product or resource is suitable.

State what was evaluated and how. Hands-on reviews require real use and relevant observations. A document review can assess clarity, completeness and usefulness from the text while identifying its evidence limits. Set criteria before the verdict and discuss shortcomings that affect the reader's decision.

Useful shape: verdict and audience, method, useful strengths, consequential limits, recommendation with conditions.

Quality question: can the reader distinguish observation, supplier claims and untested behavior? Avoid pretending documentation is hands-on testing or burying meaningful limitations.

Read the complete [Google guidance document review](examples/review.md).

## News analysis

**Reader task:** understand a real change, its significance and what to do next.

Give the event date and reporting cutoff. Attribute announcements and distinguish preview, rollout, availability and independent observation. Explain consequences for a defined audience, separating implications from predictions. Include counterevidence that changes the interpretation.

Useful shape: event, confirmed details, practical consequence, uncertainty, proportionate next action.

Quality question: could the reader repeat the central fact without confusing an announcement with access or a prediction with a result? Avoid invented urgency and a press-release rewrite without analysis.

Read the complete [Bing reporting analysis](examples/news-analysis.md).

## Opinion

**Reader task:** examine a reasoned position.

Use the author's actual stance and supplied experience. Verify the facts supporting the argument. Give a relevant objection a fair hearing, explain the tradeoff and make the consequence of adopting the position clear. Narrative headings and a direct argument may fit better than a reference layout.

Useful shape: position, reasoning or genuine experience, serious objection, response, practical consequence.

Quality question: is the stance attributable to its real author, and does the reasoning hold without invented anecdotes? Avoid assigned politics, empirical predictions disguised as preferences and removing emotion solely to sound professional.

## Project update

**Reader task:** understand what changed, what works and what remains.

Use actual artifacts and a reporting date. Separate implementation, verified behavior, proposed follow-up and acceptance. Explain a meaningful decision or tradeoff instead of listing every task. Share contributor identities and project details only when appropriate and authorized.

Useful shape: delivered change, evidence, decision and consequence, limitations, next useful step.

Quality question: does each completion claim match an inspectable artifact or observed result? Avoid fabricated release metrics, treating a build as acceptance and publishing private records to make a story feel authentic.
